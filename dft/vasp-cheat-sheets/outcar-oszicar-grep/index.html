Reading VASP output

Where VASP writes each result and the one-line commands that pull it out of OUTCAR, OSZICAR and vasprun.xml, tested on real output files from VASP 5.2 to 6.3. Includes which energy to report and what every OSZICAR column means.

The short version

  1. Use grep "free energy TOTEN" with two and three spaces: a plain grep TOTEN also matches every SCF step.
  2. A relaxation is done only if OUTCAR says reached required accuracy and its last SCF converged.
  3. Take the last occurrence of anything printed per step: energies, forces, stress, moments.
  4. Forces and stress belong to the free energy TOTEN (OSZICAR F=); with Gaussian smearing, report energy(sigma->0) (E0=).
  5. Error boxes, above all the last one before a crash, are reliably only in the standard output (your vasp.out); some warnings are also copied to OUTCAR. Keep the standard output.

Find a command

Type what you want to know, such as fermi, force, magnetic or converged, and jump to the command.

Energy: which number to report

OUTCAR labelOSZICARWhat it is
free energy TOTENF=The free energy E − TS. Forces and stress are consistent with it: use it for relaxations, phonons and NEB profiles at fixed smearing.
energy(sigma->0)E0=Extrapolated to zero smearing; the number to report with Gaussian smearing (ISMEAR = 0), provided SIGMA is small.
energy without entropy—E without the TS term; rarely reported.

With the tetrahedron method (ISMEAR = −5) all three are equal. Compare energies only between runs with the same ENCUT, PREC, LREAL, smearing and POTCAR files.

In VASP 5 vasprun.xml the e_0_energy and e_wo_entrp labels of each ionic step are swapped; read those from OUTCAR instead.

Did it converge?

An ionic step that used NELM SCF steps (60 by default) did not converge. VASP 6 also prints a warning box saying the electronic self-consistency was not achieved within NELM steps.

Forces and stress

The block lists every atom, including those fixed by selective dynamics: leave those out before comparing with EDIFFG. A total drift comparable to |EDIFFG| means noisy forces.

A positive diagonal value means the cell is compressed and wants to expand; 1 kB = 0.1 GPa. With ISIF = 1 only the pressure is valid; hybrid runs compute no stress unless ISIF is set.

Fermi energy, gap, bands and k-points

Take it from the self-consistent run, not from a band-structure path. For a semiconductor the default EFERMI = LEGACY places it somewhere in the gap; EFERMI = MIDGAP (VASP 6.4 and later) puts it in the middle.

From VASP 6.4.3 the gap report is controlled by the BANDGAP tag. On older versions compute it from vasprun.xml with pymatgen or py4vasp (below), not from DOS edges or the Fermi energy.

Magnetic moments

Site moments need LORBIT ≥ 10 (LORBIT = 11 also splits them by orbital), or RWIGS. They are integrated inside spheres around the atoms, so they do not add up to the cell moment mag=. The block is printed more than once per step: take the last. Noncollinear runs add magnetization (y) and (z) blocks.

Common mistakes

  • grep TOTEN | head: an SCF-step energy, not an ionic result.
  • Calling a relaxation converged from reached required accuracy alone, without checking its last SCF.
  • Reading the first magnetization block instead of the last, or expecting site moments to add up to mag=.
  • Taking the Fermi energy or the gap from a band-structure (path) run.
  • Reporting energy(sigma->0) from a Methfessel–Paxton run, or comparing energies with different SIGMA, ENCUT or POTCARs.
  • Not keeping the standard output, where VASP prints its error boxes.
  • Sharing vaspout.h5 publicly: in current versions it contains a copy of your POTCAR.
