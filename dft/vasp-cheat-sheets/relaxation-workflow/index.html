VASP relaxation workflow

How to take a structure from its starting guess to a converged geometry and a trustworthy energy: which optimizer and ISIF to use, when the relaxation really is finished, why the cutoff matters for the cell, and the static and DOS runs that follow.

The short version

  1. Relax with IBRION = 2, ISIF = 3 (bulk) or ISIF = 2 (slabs, defects), and a force criterion such as EDIFFG = -0.02.
  2. When the cell changes, use ENCUT ≥ 1.3 × the largest ENMAX and relax again from CONTCAR until the volume stops changing (Pulay stress).
  3. Relax metals with ISMEAR = 1 ; SIGMA = 0.2, insulators with ISMEAR = 0 ; SIGMA = 0.05; keep ISMEAR = -5 for the final static run.
  4. Take the energy you report from a static run at the relaxed geometry, not from the relaxation.
  5. A relaxation keeps the symmetry of its starting structure. To reach a lower-symmetry minimum, displace atoms in POSCAR.

The workflow, step by step

  1. Pre-relax (optional). A machine-learned potential (MACE, CHGNet, VASP's own MLFF) or a coarse VASP run (PREC = Normal, fewer k-points, EDIFFG = −0.1) gets the structure close cheaply.
  2. Relax atoms and cell with the INCAR below.
  3. Relax again from CONTCAR (cp CONTCAR POSCAR), with a fresh basis set (a new run, or ISTART = 1), until the volume changes by less than about 1%. For magnets, seed MAGMOM with the final moments.
  4. Static run at the final geometry: the energy you report, and CHGCAR for the next step.
  5. DOS and band structure from that CHGCAR (see the INCAR recipes and the KPOINTS sheet).

What moves: ISIF

ISIFAtomsCell shapeVolumeUse for
0yesnonoNo stress computed (the default for MD and hybrids).
1yesnonoOnly the pressure (trace of the stress) is valid.
2yesnonoSlabs, defects, molecules: the default otherwise.
3yesyesyesFull relaxation of bulk crystals.
4yesyesnoFixed-volume points of an energy–volume curve.
5noyesnoCell shape only.
6noyesyesCell only, atoms fixed in fractional coordinates.
7nonoyesVolume only.
8yesnoyesAtoms and volume, shape fixed (VASP 6.4.1 and later).

The stress tensor is computed for ISIF 2 to 8. In OUTCAR a positive diagonal stress means the cell is compressed and wants to expand. VASP's own volume-relaxation guide recommends relaxing one degree of freedom at a time (its example uses ISIF = 7); ISIF = 3 in one go is the common practice.

Which optimizer

IBRIONMethodWhen
2Conjugate gradientThe robust default, also far from the minimum. Fine-tune the step: optimal POTIM ≈ POTIM × the trialstep printed in the output. VASP advice
1RMM-DIIS (quasi-Newton)Close to the minimum and with more than about 20 degrees of freedom; needs accurate forces (NELMIN 4 for bulk, 8 for surfaces) and is sensitive to POTIM. NFREE 10–20 for complex cases. VASP advice
3Damped molecular dynamicsFar from the minimum or for soft systems; start with SMASS ≈ 0.4 and a POTIM about twice the smallest conjugate-gradient step. VASP advice

Without IBRION, NSW > 0 runs molecular dynamics and stops at once (no default time step). With IBRION = −1 and NSW > 0 the same structure is computed NSW times.

When it stops: EDIFFG

  • A negative EDIFFG stops when every force is below |EDIFFG| (eV/Å): -0.02 for most work, -0.01 or tighter before phonons.
  • A positive value (and the default, 10 × EDIFF) stops on the energy change between ionic steps, which can leave large forces.
  • For ISIF = 3, VASP's developers state that the test also includes the stress; the documentation does not spell this out.
  • EDIFF must be much tighter than the forces you ask for: 1E-6 or smaller, otherwise the forces are noise.
  • Hitting NSW is not convergence: OUTCAR must say reached required accuracy.

Pulay stress and the cutoff

VASP keeps the number of plane waves fixed while the cell changes, so the effective cutoff drifts with the volume and the stress comes out too negative; volumes then end up too small. Two remedies:

  • Use ENCUT ≥ 1.3 × the largest ENMAX for any relaxation that changes the cell; VASP calls this the lower recommended limit. VASP advice
  • Restart from CONTCAR with a fresh basis set (a new run, or ISTART = 1) until the cell stops changing. ISTART = 2 keeps the old basis and therefore the error. VASP advice

An energy–volume curve at fixed volumes avoids the problem and works with the default cutoff. PSTRESS can absorb a measured Pulay stress, but VASP's PSTRESS and Volume-relaxation pages give opposite signs for that correction; a higher cutoff is the safer route.

Slabs, 2D sheets, E–V curves and magnets

CaseHow
Slab with vacuumISIF = 2 with the bottom layers fixed by selective dynamics (F F F). Never ISIF = 3: the vacuum collapses.
2D sheetISIF = 3 with LATTICE_CONSTRAINTS = .TRUE. .TRUE. .FALSE., the vacuum along a3 parallel to z (VASP 6.5.0 or later in practice).
Energy–volume curve5–10 volumes spanning about ±5–10% in volume, each with ISIF = 4 (atoms and shape relax at fixed volume; a negative POSCAR scale sets the volume), then fit an equation of state.
MagnetStart high-spin; reseed MAGMOM from the final moments for the next run, and keep MAGMOM in every restart so the magnetic symmetry is not lost.
r²SCANPre-relax with PBE or PBEsol and start from its WAVECAR. Use VASP 6.6.1, not 6.6.0, for spin-polarized r²SCAN.

Check it worked

Also check that the last SCF converged, that the largest force is below |EDIFFG| (command), that the moments did not collapse, and that CONTCAR has the space group you expect (spglib or pymatgen).

How Materials Project does it

Materials Project relaxes every structure twice in a row (cell and atoms, starting high-spin ferromagnetic), then runs a static calculation on a finer mesh. Its newest r²SCAN workflow first relaxes with PBEsol, which gives a cheap starting structure and density, then relaxes with r²SCAN. Workflow packages such as atomate2 also offer a machine-learned pre-relaxation before the VASP relaxation.

Common mistakes

  • NSW set and IBRION forgotten: molecular dynamics, stopping at once.
  • One ISIF = 3 run at the default ENCUT, and its energy reported.
  • Restarting a cell relaxation with ISTART = 2.
  • ISIF = 3 on a slab; ISMEAR = −5 in a metal relaxation; ISMEAR > 0 for an insulator.
  • EDIFF too loose for the EDIFFG asked for.
  • Expecting a symmetry-lowering distortion without breaking the symmetry in POSCAR.
  • Dropping MAGMOM, or changing ENCUT, between the relaxation, static and DOS runs.
