VASP magnetism cheat sheet

Everything about magnetic VASP runs: setting the starting moments, finding the magnetic ground state, reading the moments, spin–orbit coupling and anisotropy energies, constrained moments and DFT+U, with notes for rare-earth magnets. The builder writes MAGMOM from your POSCAR.

The short version

  1. ISPIN = 2 switches spin polarization on; without it MAGMOM does nothing for the moments.
  2. MAGMOM is one value per atom in POSCAR order. Start above the expected moments (1.2–1.5 ×): a high-spin start can relax down to low spin, but not the other way.
  3. An antiferromagnet needs opposite signs on its sublattices and a cell that holds them all, often a supercell.
  4. MAGMOM also sets the magnetic symmetry: keep it in every restart, or the order can be symmetrized away.
  5. Site moments need LORBIT ≥ 10 (LORBIT = 11 is the usual choice); they are integrated in spheres and do not add up to the cell moment mag=.
  6. Spin–orbit coupling and noncollinear runs use vasp_ncl, three MAGMOM numbers per atom and GGA_COMPAT = .FALSE.

Build MAGMOM from your POSCAR

Paste a POSCAR, give each species a starting moment, and choose a pattern: ferromagnetic, or antiferromagnetic planes alternating along one lattice vector. Flip any site by hand. The builder writes MAGMOM in the POSCAR order, compressed with N*value.

  • A new magnetic run (ISTART = 0) drops every symmetry operation that the MAGMOM pattern breaks, so different starting patterns can converge to different states.
  • The default, 1 μB on every atom, is a ferromagnetic start: it will not find an antiferromagnet.
  • Not magnetic is not paramagnetic: ISPIN = 1 removes the local moments altogether. Paramagnets above TC are modelled with disordered local moments, for example a magnetic special quasirandom structure.
  • NUPDOWN fixes the total moment (fixed-spin-moment scans of E(M)). With it set, the Fermi energy in OUTCAR belongs to one spin channel only.

Finding the magnetic ground state

  1. Start high-spin ferromagnetic and relax. If every moment ends below about 0.02 μB per site, the material is probably not magnetic in DFT.
  2. Enumerate collinear orderings (ferro-, antiferro-, ferrimagnetic) in supercells; pymatgen's MagneticStructureEnumerator does this, keeping the most symmetric candidates.
  3. Relax each ordering with the same ENCUT, POTCARs and k-point density, then run a static calculation on a finer mesh.
  4. Compare energies per formula unit. Such workflows find a non-ferromagnetic ground state correctly in most cases and the exact experimental ordering in a majority, and are less reliable for rare-earth compounds.

An antiferromagnet whose magnetic cell is larger than the crystallographic one needs a supercell: bcc Cr needs the 2-atom cubic cell, rocksalt NiO a rhombohedral cell doubled along [111].

Reading the moments

  • The site table is printed with LORBIT ≥ 10 (or RWIGS). It is labelled “magnetization (x)” even in collinear runs, where moments have no direction.
  • Site moments are spin moments inside spheres; the space between the spheres is missing, so they do not sum to mag=.
  • Spin–orbit runs print the orbital moments separately with LORBMOM = .TRUE.; noncollinear runs add (y) and (z) tables.

The magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) is the energy difference between magnetization directions, often micro-eV to meV. The usual route freezes a collinear density and adds spin–orbit coupling for each direction:

The MAE is E(direction) − E(easy axis), from the free energies of the step-2 runs. Converge the MAE itself, not just the total energy, with respect to the k-points; it usually needs far denser meshes than the energy. Where spin–orbit coupling is strong (open 4f shells), compare with self-consistent spin–orbit runs.

The penalty energy (E_p in OSZICAR) must end up small before the energy means anything.

Exchange constants J come from mapping the energies of several orderings onto a Heisenberg model, E = E0 − Σ Jij ei · ej, and solving (or least-squares fitting) for the J. State the convention you used: unit vectors or spins S, each pair counted once or twice, and which sign means ferromagnetic. Each configuration must be converged in k-points on its own.

  • DFT+U has many self-consistent solutions, which can differ by hundreds of meV per atom. Ramping U up gradually, or trying several starts, helps find the lowest.
  • For a hard magnetic DFT+U case, VASP's recipe runs three steps from the previous WAVECAR: ICHARG = 12 with ALGO = Normal and no U; then ALGO = All with TIME = 0.05; then the U tags.
  • Energies with different U values cannot be compared; formation energies mixing +U and plain GGA need a correction scheme.

Rare-earth magnets

  • With f-in-core potentials (Nd_3, Sm_3) the 4f moment and the 4f single-ion anisotropy, the dominant part of the MAE in Nd₂Fe₁₄B or SmCo₅, are absent; the result is essentially the transition-metal part.
  • Studies that need the 4f contribution put the f electrons in the valence (potpaw.64 Nd or Nd_h), add U on the 4f states and spin–orbit coupling, and handle the many metastable 4f occupations explicitly.
  • Use LMAXMIX = 6 and LASPH = .TRUE. with valence f electrons. The POTCAR sheet covers the choice.

Common mistakes

  • MAGMOM without ISPIN = 2; MAGMOM count or order not matching POSCAR after building a supercell.
  • An antiferromagnet in a cell too small to hold its order.
  • Dropping MAGMOM on a restart or in the DOS run.
  • Reading the “tot” row of the site table as the cell moment.
  • Comparing energies with different U, POTCARs, ENCUT or k-point density.
  • MAE runs with the default GGA_COMPAT, LMAXMIX = 2, symmetry on, a copied WAVECAR, or NBANDS not doubled.
  • Writing MAGMOM in Cartesian x, y, z when SAXIS is not 0 0 1.
  • Expecting a 4f moment or rare-earth anisotropy from _3 potentials.
  • Taking the Fermi energy from a NUPDOWN run as the Fermi level of the material.
